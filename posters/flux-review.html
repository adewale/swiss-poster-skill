The FLUX Review, 2021–2026

Since May 2021, a “ragtag band of systems thinkers” has sent a weekly issue: an essay, about five news items called Signposts, and usually a Lens of the week. In 2021, more than a quarter of the Signposts were about COVID. From 2023, close to a third were about AI, and by 2026 so were nearly a third of the essays.

Share of each year’s items

AI’s share of the Signposts rose from 2% in 2021 to 28% in 2023, about where COVID had been in 2021. The lead essays did not reach that share until 2026.

What the essays were about

2021 · Ep. 01–32COVID 27%

To thrive in fluxEp. 22

Written in October 2021, as COVID cases climbed again, Ep. 22 asks readers to learn their “flux budget”: how much unpredictability they can take. Ep. 13 reads outrage as a sign that a story is using you as its “memetic host”.

“With exponential curves, once you see the change, it’s almost always too late”Ep. 18

2022 · Ep. 33–80Crypto 17%

Lens hygieneEp. 52

Although crypto led the tracked news, the essays turned to the lenses themselves. Ep. 52 sorts a team’s lenses into a 2×2, with a “parts bin” of unused ones for when the team must reorient. Ep. 79 calls slide decks “at best” sketches of strategy.

“…when you don’t know what lenses you’re wearing, you cannot distinguish the effect of the lens from reality.”Ep. 54

2023–24 · Ep. 81–171AI 28–32%

Trust is a long gameEp. 109

After ChatGPT, AI outgrew COVID and crypto in the Signposts, yet only six lead essays in two years were about it. Ep. 137 prices distrust with a leaky gutter: several quotes, then weeks of waiting. Ep. 152 uses schadenfreude as a tripwire for flattening people into cartoons.

“One way to think about LLMs is as upscalers of vibes.”Ep. 143

2025–26 · Ep. 172–252AI 29–31%

No neck to wringEp. 223

AI moved into the essays: 20 of the 81 written since January 2025. Ep. 231 notes that orders like “Increase velocity” would fail as AI prompts; people guess instead, and the guesses compound. Ep. 223 asks who answers for an agent’s mistake after its session closes.

“Accountability has always required something persistent to be held against.”Ep. 223
